\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:results}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Models and budgets.}
All ablations share one budget of 197{,}400 optimizer steps at global batch 32
(600 epochs of one view per plant over the 10{,}500 training plants), one
view-sampling seed, and the base ViT (114\,M parameters) unless model size is
the variable; arms that change the number of training plants keep the step
budget by changing their epoch count (Sec.~\ref{sec:method-pretrain}). The headline
model differs in schedule (1{,}000 epochs over all ten views at global batch
256) and in its point-cloud loss, which adds the Sinkhorn term of
Sec.~\ref{sec:method-pretrain}. Like the four-stream ablation arm, it takes the
parameter stream as an encoder input under the shared masking budget; the
teacher and every student of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-distill} descend from it. \todo{The headline-derived numbers
in this draft are from the first-release, Chamfer-only headline run; the
final-release Sinkhorn run and its distillation replace them.} A maize
headline model on the same schedule and loss is queued.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Geometry is symmetric Chamfer distance in the unit-sphere frame
($\times10^{-3}$; a plant spans $\approx$1.4\,m, so 1.0 is about 7\,mm), on
the masked validation step for pretrained arms and with the source unmasked
for students. Parameters are mean absolute error on the normalized $[0,1]$
fields of hidden, non-padding leaf tokens; where a per-field number is more
telling we report \emph{skill}, $1-\mathrm{MAE}/\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{mean}}$,
so that 1 is exact and $\le 0$ is no better than the average plant. Phenotype
readout follows Sec.~\ref{sec:field}: ridge regression on the frozen CLS token
with the parameter tensor zeroed, one deterministic view per plant, reported as
$R^2$ on the extreme-enriched held-out plants (2{,}250 val, 2{,}250 test; val
unless stated) with paired bootstrap intervals over plants (2{,}000 resamples).
A difference is a \emph{win} only when its interval excludes zero on val
\emph{and} test. Sorghum's four named traits have rank two by construction
(height and leaf count are one generator variable, $r{=}0.994$, and the biomass
proxy is that variable again), so we add the independent axes the generator
varies, leaf roll and leaf length; maize exposes eleven largely independent
traits (Sec.~\ref{sec:method-data}). Implementation details, per-trait tables and
intervals are in the supplement.

Table~\ref{tab:regime1} is the deployment case the title promises: one
photograph, one depth frame or one cloud of an isolated plant, and the model

\paragraph{Viewpoint.}
The ten cameras of a plant form an exact elevation ladder
($\sin\text{elev} = -0.9 + 0.2\,i$) while every view shares one cloud and one
recipe, so the view index changes the input and nothing about the answer.
Figure~\ref{fig:views}A shows what that costs. Photograph-to-cloud error is a U
in elevation, best side-on and worst looking steeply down \emph{or} up (the
common intuition that top-down views are the hard ones is half right), and the
U is the same curve for the pretrained model and every student: distillation
lowers it by $28\,\%$ on average, improves nine of ten views, and does not
flatten it (best-to-worst ratio $2.35\to2.49$). Across the 2{,}250 test plants
the camera's distance from the horizon explains $\eta^2 = 0.24$--$0.31$ of the
per-plant error, and no measure of the plant itself (leaf count, stem length,
slenderness or extent of the cloud) correlates beyond $|\rho| = 0.06$. In this
regime the view, not the morphology, decides how well a photograph can be
inverted. The recipe is the more view-stable output: ten photographs of one
plant move its recovered insertion heights by $0.08$ of the between-leaf spread
and its branching angles by $0.12$, and distillation halves their error
($-59$ and $-60\,\%$); the zero-skill waviness fields went the other way
(error flat, view spread $0.18\to0.26$), i.e.\ a field nothing can predict is
not free to carry.

\paragraph{Self-occlusion.}
Training clouds are complete; a rig's are not. Table~\ref{tab:views} scores
each model on the subset of points one or three cameras actually see, which
is half or three quarters of a sorghum plant. The information survives: refit
per condition, a model that sees RGB loses only $0.01$ to $0.03$ of height
$R^2$ at one camera and under $0.01$ at three (PC-only $-0.09$ and $-0.04$);
roll and leaf length lose $0.02$ to $0.06$. Depth matters more on a partial
cloud than on a complete one (at one camera PC+RGB loses
$-0.027\ [-0.030,-0.024]$ on height, PC+RGB+D $-0.017\ [-0.019,-0.015]$).
But the latent moves with coverage: a readout fitted on complete clouds and
transferred to one-camera clouds collapses (headline height
$0.983 \to 0.670 \to -0.27$), and refitting recovers $0.964$. The decoder
does not complete either: at one camera the distance from the true cloud to
the prediction is $8.7$ to $10.4$ against $4.0$ for echoing the partial input,
so the model reproduces what it is given rather than hallucinating the hidden
half. Both facts set the real-data protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-field}: the
probe is refit on scans, and the transfer gap is reported as the predicted
sim-to-real penalty.

\paragraph{Morphological complexity.}
The held-out splits are enriched with unusual morphologies by construction
(Sec.~\ref{sec:method-data}), and across them neither reconstruction nor the probe
degrades with plant complexity: the per-plant photograph-to-cloud error is
uncorrelated with leaf count and stem length ($|\rho| \le 0.06$ above), and
the probe's residuals on the extreme-enriched plants are what the intervals in
every table already integrate over. \todo{Fig.~4 companion: error by leaf-count
decile for both species from the \FieldProc{} evaluation; maize self-occlusion
rows of Tab.~\ref{tab:views}.}

The row data pairs every observation with an isolated twin (Sec.~\ref{sec:method-data}),
so Tab.~\ref{tab:rows} is a within-plant comparison: the only thing that
changes between its two columns is the neighbors. Three questions it answers.
How much does the zero-shot model lose when a third of its pixels and two
thirds of its cloud belong to other plants (the echo test: Chamfer against the
input cloud tells whether it reconstructs the target or the scene)? Does
training against the target plant behind its neighbors recover it, and does
the isolated control, which sees the same plants without neighbors, gain the
same (which would make the gain a data effect, not an occlusion effect)? And
does the cost follow the occlusion measure, by yaw ($13\,\%$ of the target
hidden broadside against $1\,\%$ edge-on) and by quartile? Two predictions from
Sec.~\ref{sec:regime2}: inputs that already see RGB lose least, and the
decoder will not complete a hidden blade, so the recipe, which re-renders the
whole plant, is the output that survives. \todo{Fig.~5: one plant isolated and

The pretrained model already generates across modalities without any
single-sensor training: in sorghum a photograph alone yields the cloud at
Chamfer $1.04$ and the recipe at MAE $0.034$, and the same holds in maize
($1.80$, $0.078$). Distillation against the full-modality teacher then improves
every source, and concentrates the gain where the source was weakest
(Tab.~\ref{tab:anysubset}). The mean generation loss falls $19.1\,\%$ in sorghum;
RGB and depth, already strong, gain $11\,\%$ ($-15$ and $-23\,\%$ in Chamfer),
while the cloud as the only input gains $27.5\,\%$, its Chamfer $-56.6\,\%$ and
its recipe error $-43.8\,\%$ ($0.062 \to 0.035$); from the recipe alone the
cloud improves $-62\,\%$. Maize repeats the pattern at $-20.7\,\%$ after 55
epochs, with the cloud-only row the largest move ($51.7 \to 1.97$): that
before-number is the collapse of a model fed an unmasked cloud it never saw in
pretraining, the failure the teacher protocol of Sec.~\ref{sec:distill} was
designed around, and the reason per-source numbers are reported rather than
only the mean.

Three controls bound the result. Two seeds of the RGB-only student agree to the
third digit (test $1.00 \to 0.79$ for both; val $1.01 \to 0.80$). Hiding half
of the source's own tokens during distillation beats full visibility
($0.80$ vs.\ $0.85$ val), and Fig.~\ref{fig:views}B shows where: the easiest
quartile of plants loses $3\,\%$, the hardest gains $12\,\%$, so the
regularizer buys robustness, not average-case polish. And a specialist
distilled on the recipe alone from the cloud reaches MAE $0.039$ ($-37\,\%$),
the four-target generalist $0.035$ ($-44\,\%$): the other heads act as auxiliary
supervision for parameter regression rather than competing with it
(\todo{the two ran at different learning rates, $3\!\times\!10^{-5}$ vs.\
$10^{-4}$; the matched-LR specialist is queued}).

stem radius $0.29\!\to\!0.75$). A cloud carries the silhouette but not the
shading, overlap and texture cues that place organs on the stem.
\textbf{Depth is a reconstruction win and a phenotype wash:} it lowers sorghum
Chamfer by $32\,\%$ and maize by $25\,\%$ but moves the sorghum probe by at
most $+0.01$ on size and $+0.09$ on leaf length; on maize (final release,
paired over plants) depth wins no trait and loses three on both splits, tassel
droop ($-0.10$ to $-0.12$), curl ($-0.06$ to $-0.10$) and twist ($-0.03$), with
the other eight ties within $0.015$.
\textbf{The parameter stream, used as an input, does not help.} On sorghum the
four-stream arm loses to PC+RGB+D on every learnable target on both splits
(size $-0.015$ to $-0.017$, roll $-0.013$, leaf length $-0.082$; all six
intervals exclude zero), and its Chamfer gives back exactly what depth gained.
On the first maize release the same arm lost 10 of 11 traits; on the final
release the paired comparison is a draw (PC+RGB+D wins droop $+0.04$ to
$+0.07$, loses curl $-0.04$ to $-0.07$ and twist $-0.035$, and the eight
remaining traits tie within $0.02$: height $0.635$ vs.\ $0.633$, stem radius
$0.743$ vs.\ $0.760$) while the four-stream arm wins the Chamfer; against
PC+RGB it loses droop ($-0.05$) and tassel droop ($-0.13$) and ties the rest. Either way, the stream that
encodes the answer verbatim in training is not the stream that makes the
frozen feature better at deployment, where it is absent.

A fourth stream at mask ratio $0.8$ splits the visible budget four ways, so
Tab.~\ref{tab:e2} alone cannot separate a token-budget handicap from a
representational one: as trained, the four-stream arm's sensors see $107.4$
visible tokens against $117.0$ and its parameter tokens are $58\,\%$ visible.
Table~\ref{tab:control} removes the handicap. Restoring the sensor budget does
not recover the three-stream probe at any parameter visibility; the trend with
visibility is monotone on the size targets ($60\,\% < 20\,\% < 0\,\%$ visible),
and making the parameters a pure reconstruction target still costs $-0.011$ on
size, $-0.07$ on roll and $-0.10$ on leaf length. The two traits the leaf
tokens spell out per leaf lose most, which is consistent with the encoder
routing them through tokens that will not exist in the field, and the
target-only result says the reconstruction pressure alone does part of the
damage. The headline model and its teacher were nevertheless trained with the
parameters in the encoder's shared budget (Sec.~\ref{sec:method-pretrain}), as in the
four-stream arm of Tab.~\ref{tab:e2}; the ablations predict that its frozen
representation is weaker than a three-stream or $\rho_\theta{=}1$ model would
give, which we have not tested at the headline scale. Supervising the
parameters as a target is, in turn, what makes the generalist beat the
parameter specialist (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-distill}).

\subsection{What the metrics disagree about}
\label{sec:res-metrics}\label{sec:exp-metrics}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/fig_metrics.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Chamfer against the frozen probe, twelve sorghum arms}
  (streams, parameter controls, training plants, model sizes; val). The
  metrics agree on the gross order and disagree on the decisions: depth
  (PC+RGB $\to$ PC+RGB+D) moves Chamfer $-32\,\%$ and height $R^2$ by
  $+0.003$; more plants (1k $\to$ 10k) moves Chamfer $-20\,\%$ and the
  probe not at all; the parameter stream returns the Chamfer of PC+RGB with a
  worse probe.}
  \label{fig:metrics}

% figures/make_metric_fig.py from the tables above
Chamfer and the probe rank the arms alike only in the coarse sense
(Fig.~\ref{fig:metrics}; Spearman $-0.57$ to $-0.88$ across twelve arms), and
every design decision in this paper falls in the region where they part.
Depth is the clearest case: $-32\,\%$ Chamfer in sorghum, $-25\,\%$ in maize,
and a probe that does not move (Sec.~\ref{sec:param-stream}). Data is the
second: sorghum Chamfer keeps improving from 1{,}000 to 10{,}000 plants
($-20\,\%$) while every size target sits at $R^2$ $0.96$ from 1{,}000 plants
up (Tab.~\ref{tab:scaling}), and in maize Chamfer falls fourfold while the
probe is not even monotone. The parameter stream is the third: on the final
maize release it gives the best Chamfer of the four arms and no probe gain. And
the partial clouds of Tab.~\ref{tab:views} are the fourth, in the other
direction: at one camera Chamfer is $5$ to $7\times$ worse while the refit probe
loses at most $0.03$. Point-MAE closes the list from outside: it beats our
PC-only arm on every sorghum trait under the probe (Tab.~\ref{tab:e8}), so our
lead is not a better cloud encoder but the fusion of a photograph with geometry.

The two quantities differ in what they can see. A nearest-neighbour distance
is satisfied by surface proximity and lets many predicted points share one
target point, so it rewards a decoder that lands on the bulk of a slender plant
and is nearly blind to a missing or merged blade~\cite{wu2021dcd,song2026structural};
the probe reads the structure the encoder kept, which is what a trait pipeline
consumes. We therefore make the probe the deciding metric for modality and data
decisions, report Chamfer as the decoder's surface error that it is, and
answer the training-side half of the problem with the Sinkhorn term of
Sec.~\ref{sec:method-pretrain}, which matches the two clouds as distributions and
cannot be satisfied by a clump. \todo{Add the E7 ablation of that term, and
F-score at 1\,cm beside Chamfer in Tabs.~\ref{tab:regime1} and
\ref{tab:anysubset}.}

\paragraph{Against published encoders.}
Table~\ref{tab:e8} reads four public checkpoints and a supervised network
through the same probe. On sorghum the three-stream \CropCode{} wins every
learnable trait against every baseline; against the strongest, EmbodiedMAE-B,
the paired margins are $+0.005$ on size (tight intervals at a $0.98$ ceiling),
$+0.08$ on roll and $+0.10$ on leaf length. The four-stream arm loses the size
traits to EmbodiedMAE-B by $0.005$ to $0.007$: the parameter-stream penalty of
Sec.~\ref{sec:param-stream} measured against an external model. A network of
our architecture trained from scratch on the probe targets reaches $0.974$
height and $0.811$ roll through its own head, and the frozen three-stream
feature beats it on six of seven traits (leaf length $+0.08$ to $+0.12$) while
the frozen four-stream feature loses size and both angles. Pretraining pays
without the parameter stream; with it, the frozen feature is slightly worse
than no pretraining. On the first maize release \CropCode{} beats EmbodiedMAE-B
on 9 of 11 traits and the best baseline per trait on 5, losing leaf count and
curl to Point-MAE and tassel droop to DINOv2, the one trait where every
EmbodiedMAE-design encoder is below zero.

\paragraph{Camera pose, a forensic note.}
Point-MAE given each view's full camera pose beats our camera-frame four-stream
arm on 8 of 11 maize traits (height $0.720$ vs.\ $0.597$). Most of that is a
generator artefact: the maize renderer puts every plant's leaf plane in the
same world plane, so a full pose rotation hands the encoder a canonical plant
azimuth no rig provides. With gravity only (the camera levelled, its azimuth
kept, what a calibrated rig knows), Point-MAE's height falls to $0.588$ and
camera-frame PC+RGB+D wins five traits (height, biomass, twist, width, stem
radius $+0.23$) and loses four (leaf angle, leaf count, curl $-0.18$, tassel
droop $-0.21$). Our fused arms gain nothing from levelled clouds; PC-only
gains five. A generator's unintended regularities are a benchmark hazard, and
this one was found only because the baseline was too good.

\paragraph{Data, capacity, and the noise floor.}
Two near-duplicate runs, 10{,}000 and 10{,}500 training plants, differ by
$3\,\%$ in Chamfer and $0.004$ in probe $R^2$; we take that as the floor.
Against it (Tab.~\ref{tab:scaling}), sorghum reconstruction improves from
1{,}000 to 3{,}000 plants ($-14\,\%$, five times the floor) and more weakly to
10{,}000 ($-7\,\%$), while the probe is flat: the size traits sit at $0.96$
from 1{,}000 plants up and only leaf length moves ($0.27\!\to\!0.30$).
Capacity buys more than data at this budget: small to base is $-17\,\%$ in
Chamfer and $+0.02$ to $+0.03$ on every probe trait, and large adds nothing to
Chamfer and $+0.01$ to the probe, so base is the knee. Maize is noisier: Chamfer
falls fourfold with data but the probe is not monotone (height $0.39 / 0.52 /
0.43 / 0.64$), and the three sizes are within $0.05$ on every trait. The
paired bootstrap says the dip is not sampling noise: every reduced arm loses
to the full-data arm on 10 of 11 traits on both splits (twist ties), by $0.03$
to $0.24$, and the 3{,}000-plant arm beats the 10{,}000-plant arm on height
($+0.10$), leaf length ($+0.10$) and leaf count ($+0.04$) while losing stem
radius ($-0.10$), each interval excluding zero on val and test. With one seed
per arm the remaining candidate is run-to-run variance of the arm itself.

\paragraph{What the latent encodes.}
% reports/e9_pr_600_16592054/, reports/e9_pr_tg100_600_16686383/, reports/e9_pm_600_22751615/
There is no genotype in either generator: every plant is one draw from one
continuous distribution, so a clustered latent would be a defect, not a
finding. The frozen CLS space is a continuum (best silhouette $0.15$ to $0.24$
at $k{=}2$, adjusted Rand index $\approx 0$ against shape deciles, t-SNE
trustworthiness $0.95$ to $0.99$) of low effective dimension: participation
ratio $4.4$ to $6.3$ of 768 in sorghum and $6.7$ to $11.3$ in maize, 14 to 15
principal components for $90\,\%$ of the variance once RGB is present in
sorghum and 18 to 19 in maize, in step with maize's more independent generative
factors. In sorghum its leading direction is the plant's size (first component
vs.\ leaf count $|r| = 0.73$ to $0.94$ across the arms); in maize no single
component carries a trait (best single-component correlation with leaf count
$|r| \le 0.17$ in every arm, although the probe reads leaf count at $R^2 = 0.91$
from the same features), so the maize traits are spread over many directions.
The parameter stream bends the sorghum geometry the way the probe predicts: the gated arms are the least size-aligned ($|r|$ $0.48$ and
$0.34$) and most isotropic (participation ratio $8.5$ and $14.3$), the
target-only arm the most size-dominated of the five (first component $43\,\%$
of variance, $|r| = 0.95$). Linear decodability also dissociates from local
embedding structure: roll reads at $R^2$ $0.81$ from a latent in which it is
barely locally organized, which is why we treat the probe, not t-SNE
smoothness, as the instrument for representation claims on a continuous
generator. \todo{E9 figure: t-SNE of the three- and four-stream latents colored
by leaf count and by roll.}
