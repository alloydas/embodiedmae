\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\upd{In this section, we present \method{}, a multimodal framework that recovers
editable procedural plant representations from incomplete or degraded sensor
observations.} \upd{We first formulate the inverse reconstruction task and describe
our procedural field-data generation engine.} \upd{Next, we introduce the
four-stream token representations and masked autoencoding pretraining
objective.} \upd{Finally, we detail our teacher--student distillation strategy for
single-sensor deployment and the evaluation protocols used to assess model
transfer under field occlusion.} \upd{An overview of the framework is illustrated
in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}.}

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:method-form}

\upd{Let $G$ denote a procedural generator that maps a parameter vector
$\theta \in \R^{d_\theta}$ to a complete 3D plant mesh $G(\theta)$.} \upd{The
parameters describe plant-level attributes, including stem geometry,
alongside leaf-level parameters such as insertion height, length, roll,
branching angle, and blade width.} \upd{We consider three sensor modalities,
$\mathcal{M} = \{\mathrm{rgb}, \mathrm{d}, \mathrm{pc}\}$, corresponding to
an RGB photograph, a depth frame, and a 3D point cloud.} \upd{In field campaigns,
only an incomplete subset $S \subseteq \mathcal{M}$ may be captured per
plant, and observations are routinely degraded by partial coverage or
occlusion from adjacent row neighbors.}

\upd{Our objective is to learn a feed-forward inverse mapping:}
\begin{equation}
  f_\phi\!\left(\{x^m : m \in S\}\right)
  = \left(\hat\theta, \{\hat x^m : m \in \mathcal{M}\}\right),
  \label{eq:procedural-inverse}
\end{equation}
\upd{where $\phi$ represents the network parameters, $\hat\theta$ is the
predicted procedural description, and $\hat x^m$ denotes the reconstructed
sensor modalities.} \upd{The predicted output $G(\hat\theta)$ provides both
editable 3D geometry and direct phenotypic trait extraction within a single
compact representation.} \upd{Unlike per-instance fitting pipelines, which
optimize each plant separately, \method{} learns a shared inverse mapping
executed in a single forward pass.}

\upd{The procedural generator provides both synthetic sensor observations and
exact parameter ground truth, enabling supervised learning without manual
annotation.} \upd{At deployment, $\theta$ is unknown and must be inferred strictly
from available sensor inputs.} \upd{We therefore distinguish the parameter
stream's role as a decoder reconstruction target from its privileged use as
an encoder input during training, controlling its visibility to evaluate its
impact on representation quality (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-params}).}

\paragraph{Procedural generation.}
\upd{We construct \dataset{} using species-specific procedural generators for
maize and sorghum, creating 15{,}000 distinct plants per species
(Tab.~\ref{tab:fieldproc}).} \upd{Each plant is generated from a unique seed
paired with exact ground-truth parameters.} \upd{Maize parameters are sampled from
Gaussian distributions (e.g., height $0.83 \pm 0.10$\,m, leaf count
$12.4 \pm 2.6$, leaf angle $44^\circ \pm 10^\circ$) coupled via four latent
traits (vigor, slenderness, posture, texture) with per-leaf canopy position
curves and spatial jitter.} \upd{Sorghum plants are generated using the EvoEngine
sorghum generator, drawing stem tilt, heading, leaf count, and internode
spacing to evaluate continuous per-leaf curves for insertion height, length,
width, roll, and branching angle.}

\upd{The two generators feature distinct morphological trait dependencies:
sorghum stem length is strongly coupled to leaf count
($\text{stem length} = 0.05\,n_{\text{leaves}} - 0.001$, $R^2 = 0.988$),
whereas maize leaf count and plant height are loosely correlated
($r = 0.20$), allowing us to test framework generalization across structural
regimes.}

\paragraph{Isolated-plant observations.}
\upd{For each synthetic plant, we render ten camera perspectives at
$1024 \times 1024$ resolution ($40^\circ$ vertical field of view), capturing
RGB photos, losslessly packed 32-bit depth frames, and exact camera poses.} \upd{Sorghum uses a fixed Fibonacci-sphere rig at 2.5\,m distance (elevations
$-64^\circ$ to $+64^\circ$), and each view includes the occlusion-free
whole-plant point cloud expressed in the camera frame.} \upd{Maize uses ten view
directions drawn uniformly on the sphere per plant, at a
bounding-box-fitted distance, with an 8{,}192-point cloud back-projected
from that view's depth frame, so the cloud holds the camera-visible surface
geometry.}

\paragraph{Row observations.}
\upd{To model realistic field occlusion, each target plant is rendered within a
simulated crop row flanked by two adjacent neighbors from the same split at
0.15\,m stem spacing.} \upd{Rows are rendered across ten orientation angles
($\theta \in \{0^\circ, 10^\circ, \dots, 90^\circ\}$) with uniform random
neighbor offsets ($\pm 15^\circ$).} \upd{A contact solver prevents mesh
intersection while keeping the plants close to their grown shapes: each leaf
blade is modeled as an articulated chain of 8\,cm segments that bend
smoothly along circular arcs upon inter-plant contact.}

\upd{A level ground-rover camera captures full row scenes (RGBA, depth, and a
24{,}576-point scene cloud holding both target and neighbor geometry)
alongside a ground-truth capture of the target plant alone in its
contact-bent shape (8{,}192 points).} \upd{In maize, neighbors account for
59--68\% of the plant pixels in the frame and two thirds of the input point
cloud; in sorghum, 23\% of the target's pixels are occluded behind neighbors
on average across all orientation angles.}

\paragraph{Data partitioning.}
\upd{Plants are partitioned into training, validation, and test sets using a
70/15/15 ratio by plant instance ($10{,}500$ train / $2{,}250$ val /
$2{,}250$ test), ensuring all views and row orientations of a given plant
remain in one split.} \upd{Held-out sets are biased toward extreme morphologies to
evaluate generalization: maize plants are partitioned via a tail split based
on Mahalanobis distance across 19 standardized parameter features, while
sorghum plants are sampled along a Mahalanobis rank gradient across six core
morphological parameters.}

\subsection{Four-stream masked pretraining}
\label{sec:method-pretrain}

\paragraph{Token representations and architecture.}
\upd{Our architecture extends EmbodiedMAE~\cite{dong2025embodiedmae} to
incorporate structured procedural parameter streams alongside standard
visual inputs.} \upd{RGB images and depth maps are divided into 196 patch tokens
on a $14 \times 14$ grid with 2D sine--cosine positional embeddings.} \upd{Point
clouds are processed by selecting 196 group centers via farthest point
sampling (FPS), grouping 32 nearest neighbors per center, and embedding
local point sets using a PointNet encoder~\cite{qi2017pointnet}.}

\upd{The procedural parameter stream consists of $1+K$ tokens: one plant-level
attribute token and $K$ individual leaf tokens ($K=28$ for maize, $K=24$ for
sorghum).} \upd{Parameters are affinely normalized to $[0,1]$ and projected
linearly as continuous number tokens via xVal-style numerical
encoding~\cite{golkar2023xval}.} \upd{Learned modality embeddings distinguish
streams, and a trainable \texttt{[CLS]} token is prepended to the visible
sequence.}

\upd{A 12-block ViT encoder (width 768) processes visible tokens, and an 8-block
decoder (width 512) reconstructs all $L = 588 + (1+K)$ token positions using
learnable mask tokens for missing inputs.} \upd{Linear heads predict RGB and depth
patches, a FoldingNet head outputs 41 points per point-cloud token, and an
MLP regresses continuous procedural parameters.} \upd{When predicted parameters
are scored or re-rendered, the real leaf slots are selected by the
ground-truth validity mask; \todo{inferring leaf presence from the sensors
alone is left to future work}.}

\paragraph{Modality masking.}
\upd{We apply a total mask ratio of $\rho = 0.80$ across all four streams,
yielding a visible token budget}
\begin{equation}
  V = \left\lfloor (1-\rho)\,L \right\rfloor,
  \label{eq:visible-budget}
\end{equation}
\upd{corresponding to 122 visible tokens for sorghum ($L=613$) and 123 for maize
($L=617$).} \upd{At each training step, $V$ is distributed dynamically across
streams by sampling from a $\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\mathbf{1})$ distribution,
ensuring each stream maintains at least one visible token and at least 25\%
masked tokens.} \upd{In the default pretraining setup, parameter tokens are
included in this budget, rendering them approximately 58\% visible on
average.}

\upd{To evaluate parameter stream placement (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-params}), we also
train control variants where parameter tokens are uncoupled from the sensor
budget and masked at fixed visibility ratios $\rho_\theta$:}
\begin{equation}
 V_\theta =
 \begin{cases}
  0, & \rho_\theta = 1,\\
  \max\!\left(1, \left\lfloor (1+K)(1-\rho_\theta) \right\rfloor\right), & 0 \le \rho_\theta < 1.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:parameter-visibility}
\end{equation}
\upd{Setting $\rho_\theta = 1$ forces procedural parameters to serve strictly as
a decoder reconstruction target without entering the encoder.}

\paragraph{Reconstruction objective.}
\upd{The overall pretraining objective combines four modality loss terms:}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} = \ell_{\mathrm{rgb}} + \ell_{\mathrm{d}} + \ell_{\mathrm{pc}} + 5\ell_\theta.
  \label{eq:reconstruction-loss}
\end{equation}
\upd{$\ell_{\mathrm{rgb}}$ is MSE on patch-normalized targets~\cite{he2022mae},
$\ell_{\mathrm{d}}$ is MSE on min--max normalized depth maps, and
$\ell_\theta$ is Smooth-$L_1$ loss over masked parameter tokens.} \upd{Point
clouds are supervised using a sigmoid-weighted Chamfer distance to mitigate
nearest-neighbor clumping on thin
blades~\cite{wu2021dcd,song2026structural}:}
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{\mathrm{pc}} = \frac{1}{|\hat P|}\sum_{p\in\hat P}w(\delta(p,P))\,\delta(p,P) + \frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{q\in P}w(\delta(q,\hat P))\,\delta(q,\hat P),
 \label{eq:point-loss}
\end{equation}
\upd{where $\delta(p,P) = \min_{q \in P} \|p - q\|_2$,
$w(r) = \sigma(a(r - \tau))$, $a = 100$, and $\tau = 0.01$.}

\upd{To encourage global structural coverage on slender geometry, the headline
model incorporates a debiased Sinkhorn divergence
term~\cite{feydy2019sinkhorn}:}
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{\mathrm{pc}}^{\star} = \ell_{\mathrm{pc}} + 0.5\,S_{\varepsilon}\!\left(\hat P_{1024}, P_{1024}\right),
  \label{eq:point-loss-sinkhorn}
\end{equation}
\upd{computed on random 1{,}024-point subsets sampled at each iteration
($\varepsilon = 0.02$).} \upd{The ablation arms of
Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments} use Eq.~\eqref{eq:point-loss} alone; the Sinkhorn
term enters the headline model and the distillation.} \upd{Models are trained
using AdamW ($\beta=(0.9, 0.95)$, weight decay 0.05, cosine decay): the
headline schedule peaks at $6 \times 10^{-4}$ over 1{,}000 epochs of all ten
views at global batch 256, while ablation arms train for a matched budget of
197{,}400 optimizer steps at global batch 32, one view per plant per epoch,
peaking at $1.5 \times 10^{-4}$.}

\subsection{Full-modality teacher to single-sensor students}
\label{sec:distill}\label{sec:method-distill}

\upd{Because masking retains at least one visible token per stream, pretraining
never presents the encoder with a single-sensor input; deployment does.} \upd{We
close this gap via teacher--student feature
distillation~\cite{hinton2015distillation}.} \upd{The teacher is a frozen
four-stream model receiving all input modalities (RGB, depth, point cloud,
and privileged parameter tokens): for sorghum every token of every stream is
visible, while for maize the teacher retains half the tokens of each stream,
since its point-cloud reconstruction degrades when fully visible.} \upd{The
teacher outputs a global \texttt{[CLS]} representation $c^T \in \R^{768}$
and normalized decoder feature maps $F^T \in \R^{L \times 512}$.}

\upd{A student model is initialized from teacher weights and trained for 100
epochs using AdamW (learning rate $10^{-4}$, global batch 128).} \upd{During
training, 80\% of steps perform single-source distillation where the student
receives only a single source
$s \in \{\mathrm{rgb}, \mathrm{d}, \mathrm{pc}, \theta\}$, while 20\% of
steps execute standard four-stream masked pretraining to prevent feature
collapse.} \upd{At deployment only sensor sources are available, and each is
evaluated alone.} \upd{The student is optimized via combined reconstruction and
cross-modal feature matching:}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{student}} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} + \frac{\lambda_F}{|\mathcal{J}_{\neg s}|} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_{\neg s}} \|F^S_j - F^T_j\|_2^2 + \lambda_c \|c^S - c^T\|_2^2,
  \label{eq:distillation-loss}
\end{equation}
\upd{where $\mathcal{J}_{\neg s}$ denotes decoder positions corresponding to
unobserved non-source streams ($\lambda_F = 1.0, \lambda_c = 0.5$).} \upd{Through
Eq.~\eqref{eq:distillation-loss}, single-sensor student models learn to
predict complete 3D geometry and continuous parameters guided by the
teacher's multi-sensor features.}

\subsection{Field evaluation and transfer protocols}
\label{sec:field}

\upd{We evaluate procedural reconstruction and representation quality under
three protocols:}

\paragraph{Phenotypic trait probing.}
\upd{To quantify morphological trait information captured by encoder latents, we
fit ridge regression probes on frozen \texttt{[CLS]} representations of the
training plants and score them on the held-out splits, one deterministic
view per plant.} \upd{Parameter tokens are zeroed during probing to prevent label
leakage.} \upd{Traits are scored using the coefficient of determination ($R^2$)
accompanied by 95\% paired bootstrap confidence intervals over plants
(2{,}000 resamples).} \upd{A modality combination is considered superior only when
its gain's confidence interval excludes zero on both validation and test
splits.} \upd{Under partial point-cloud input, the probe is either refit per
visibility condition (\emph{matched}) or applied unchanged from the
complete-cloud fit (\emph{transferred}); partial-cloud reconstruction keeps
the masked input regime of pretraining.}

\paragraph{Row occlusion protocol.}
\upd{Models are evaluated on row scenes under two setups: (1) \emph{zero-shot
transfer}, feeding row observations directly into isolated-trained models;
and (2) \emph{row fine-tuning}, maintaining the pretraining objective while
training decoders to reconstruct target main plants behind occluding row
neighbors.} \upd{A control arm runs the same fine-tuning schedule on the isolated
twins, and generated clouds are scored against the target plant and against
the input scene, so a model that merely copies the scene is caught.}

\paragraph{Real-field validation.}
\upd{Real-field performance is evaluated on laser scans from the MaizeField3D
dataset~\citep{maizefield3d2025}.} \upd{Predicted parameters and re-rendered
geometry are compared against the procedural fits of a staged
vision-language pipeline~\citep{hadadi2026vlmmaize}, which serve as
independently reconstructed ground truth.}
