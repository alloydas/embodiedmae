\section{Detailed Literature Review}
\label{app:litreview}

This appendix expands the compressed related-work discussion of
Section~\ref{sec:related} to the six bodies of work on which the paper builds
or from which it departs.

\subsection{Procedural plant models and inverse procedural modeling}
Procedural modeling of plants began with
L-systems~\citep{prusin1990abop} and matured into generators whose
parameters are organ-level, biologically meaningful
quantities~\citep{floraforge2026,hadadi2025procmaize}. Inverting such
models, that is, recovering their parameters from observations, has a
long history for woody species: Stava et al.\ fit tree parameters by stochastic
search~\citep{stava2014ipmtrees}, Talton et al.\ formulated procedural
inversion as MCMC inference~\citep{talton2011metropolis}, and Guo et al.\
inferred L-systems from point clouds~\citep{guo2020ipmlsys},
with neural variants following~\citep{latentlsys2023}. These methods
optimize or infer per instance, and none performs amortized, feed-forward
inversion of a plant generator from field-degraded input. The closest
amortized precedents are outside agriculture: shape-program
induction~\citep{shapeprograms2019}, diffusion-based inverse procedural
content generation from a single image~\citep{zhao2025dipcg}, and
cadrille, which maps images, point clouds, or text to executable CAD
programs~\citep{kolodiazhnyi2025cadrille,text2cad2024}. All operate on
rigid, isolated, clean-input objects; none addresses deformable slender
organs, inter-plant occlusion, or real-sensor noise, and none studies
whether the program parameters should participate in the encoder.

\subsection{Learned parametric recovery of crop plants}
Two 2025 systems recover parametric plants from scans. Demeter
learns a morphology model (topology, articulation, PCA shape) from real
soybean point clouds~\citep{demeter2025}; Ghrer et al.\ infer
grammar-style parameterized plants from 3D scans~\citep{ghrer2025paramplants}.
Both take a single, reasonably complete point cloud as input and neither
addresses missing modalities or row occlusion. CropCraft recovers crop
structure by Bayesian optimization of a procedural model against
multi-view NeRF depth~\citep{cropcraft2026}; the approach is accurate
but per-scene and slow. A staged pipeline annotates terrestrial
laser scans with a vision-language model and fits a NURBS procedural
descriptor by differentiable refinement~\citep{hadadi2026vlmmaize,
hadadi2025procmaize,nurbsdiff2022}; it handles isolated, occlusion-free
plants at ${\sim}10$ minutes each and serves in this paper as both the
occlusion-free baseline and the source of real-data procedural ground
truth. Tree-D Fusion reconstructs simulation-ready urban trees from
street imagery via diffusion priors~\citep{lee2024treedfusion}, but its
envelope-then-branch pipeline targets woody silhouettes, a different
geometry class from thin cereal blades.

\subsection{Point-cloud completion and slender structures}
Learned completion runs from PCN~\citep{yuan2018pcn} through
transformer approaches~\citep{yu2021pointr,yu2023adapointr} to recent
extrapolation--interpolation designs~\citep{cai2024einet}, trained and
evaluated on benchmarks of compact rigid objects. Neither
PCN's eight categories nor ShapeNet-55/34 contains a
slender-under-occlusion class, and completion systems assume the missing
geometry is self-occluded surface, without clutter from \emph{other}
objects inside the crop region. Plant-specific completion
is emerging, with amodal segmentation plus completion for occluded
plants~\citep{lee2026occ}, but it outputs geometry rather than
structure or parameters. Work on curve-aware and thin-structure
reconstruction~\citep{curvegaussian2025,qi2026rethinking} addresses the
slender regime, in which the standard metric itself fails: Chamfer
distance under-weights thin sheets~\citep{wu2021dcd}, a failure now
documented analytically~\citep{song2026structural} and addressed by density-aware
and information-theoretic variants~\citep{wu2021dcd,lin2023infocd}. We
further compare geometric metrics with frozen-latent phenotype probes and
show that they can rank modalities differently.

\subsection{3D plant phenotyping}
Organ-level 3D phenotyping datasets, including Pheno4D's labeled maize
and tomato sequences~\citep{schunck2021pheno4d},
Crops3D~\citep{crops3d2024}, and field-scale maize scans with procedural
fits~\citep{maizefield3d2025}, support trait extraction from point
clouds~\citep{qi2017pointnet,qi2017pointnetpp}. Radiance-field capture is
increasingly practical in field conditions~\citep{arshad2024nerf_field}.
No existing dataset, however, combines real field capture, slender
organs, inter-plant row occlusion, and procedural ground truth, and our
benchmark is designed to combine all four.

\subsection{Multimodal masked pretraining and distillation}
Masked autoencoding~\citep{he2022mae} extends to multiple
modalities: MultiMAE over RGB, depth, and semantics~\citep{bachmann2022multimae};
4M and 4M-21 to any-to-any generation over tokenized
modalities~\citep{mizrahi2023fourm,bachmann2024fourm21}; Point-MAE to
geometry~\citep{pang2022pointmae}; and EmbodiedMAE, on which our
architecture is based, to RGB, depth, and point clouds for robot
manipulation~\citep{dong2025embodiedmae}. Missing-modality robustness is
an active concern~\citep{wang2024robustmissing}, and cross-modal
distillation from multi-sensor teachers to single-sensor students is
established for discriminative tasks; we distill a \emph{generative}
full-modality teacher for structured-parameter regression, and encode
continuous parameters with xVal-style number
tokens~\citep{golkar2023xval}. Our finding that a privileged parameter
stream suppresses representation quality connects to modality
competition~\citep{peng2022ogm} and gradient
conflict~\citep{yu2020pcgrad,chen2018gradnorm}, which offer candidate
mechanisms; no prior work, to our knowledge, documents the effect for
generator parameters in multimodal pretraining.

\subsection{Synthetic-data pretraining and sim-to-real}
Pretraining without natural data is viable, from abstract
fractals~\citep{kataoka2020fractaldb} to procedural
worlds~\citep{raistrick2023infinigen,greff2022kubric}, and domain
randomization reduces the reality gap~\citep{tobin2017domainrand}. The value
of synthetic data is known to be task-dependent~\citep{fan2023intriguing},
and self-consumption is a documented risk~\citep{alemohammad2023mad}. Our
setting is the favorable case: the deployment target \emph{is} the
generator's parameterization, the generator spans the morphology of the
crop, and the method is evaluated on real field data.

\section{Detailed Ablation Studies}
\label{app:ablations}

All numbers in this appendix are on the final data releases of both species
unless marked as first release. Protocol: every arm trains for 197{,}400
optimizer steps at global batch 32; representation quality is a ridge probe on
the frozen CLS token, one fixed view per plant, identical target vectors across
arms, with paired bootstrap intervals over plants (a win or a loss needs the
95\,\% interval to exclude zero on both val and test); generation quality is the
validation Chamfer at the matched checkpoint.

\subsection{Token-budget control arms}
In the four-stream arm the three sensor streams share the visible budget with
the parameter tokens (107.4 visible sensor tokens against 117.0 in the
three-stream arm, parameter tokens 58\,\% visible). The control arms keep the
parameter tokens outside the shared budget, restoring the 117.0 sensor tokens,
at three parameter visibilities (20\,\%, 60\,\%, 0\,\% = target only). In sorghum
no visibility recovers the three-stream arm: height 0.959--0.966 against 0.977,
roll 0.702--0.771 against 0.781, and the 60\,\% and target-only arms lose all
nine learnable traits on both splits. In maize the 20\,\% and target-only arms
beat both the shared-budget arm and PC+RGB+D on all eleven traits, while the
60\,\% arm loses nine. Full table and intervals: Tab.~\ref{tab:control} of the
main paper.

\subsection{Parameters as a target only}
Reconstructing the parameters without ever encoding them (100\,\% masked as
input) still loses to omitting the stream in sorghum: size $-0.011$, roll
$-0.07$, mean leaf length $-0.10$, leaf width $-0.15$ (val), intervals clear
of zero. Its latent is the most size-dominated of the sorghum arms: the first
principal component carries 44\,\% of the variance and correlates with leaf
count at $|r|{=}0.95$, and the participation ratio falls to 4.3 against 6.7
for PC+RGB+D. In maize the same arm is the best four-stream variant and beats
PC+RGB+D on every trait, so the effect of the reconstruction target depends on
the generator.

\subsection{Distillation, per source modality}
From the headline model of each species, distillation lowers the mean
generation loss by 30.2\,\% in sorghum and 43.5\,\% in maize (val, epoch-0 warm
start against epoch 100). The gain is largest where the warm start is weakest:
sorghum Chamfer from the point cloud falls from $5.48$ to $1.39\times10^{-3}$
and from the parameters alone from $22.7$ to $6.83$; maize Chamfer from the
photograph falls from $11.3$ to $0.77$. The parameter error from a point cloud
alone halves in sorghum ($0.040 \to 0.020$) and changes little in maize
($0.020 \to 0.018$). On the first release, distilling all four targets beat
distilling the parameter head alone ($-43.8\,\%$ against $-36.9\,\%$ parameter
error), and distillation moved the whole view-difficulty curve down without
flattening it (9 of 10 views improved). \todo{LR caveat (first release): the
parameter specialist ran at $3\times10^{-5}$ against $10^{-4}$.}

\subsection{View invariance without labels (first release)}
Ten views of one plant share one parameter ground truth, so the spread
of ten predictions is a label-free view-invariance measure. Distillation
roughly halves error \emph{and} view spread on the fields with skill
(starting point $-59\,\%$, branching angle $-60\,\%$).
Zero-skill fields instead become \emph{more} view-dependent, as the head
starts to track image noise; the final generator drops those fields. View
stability and accuracy are separate properties: several fields move less
than 0.3 of their spread across views while remaining worse than predicting
the plant mean.

\subsection{Frozen-encoder baselines and camera-pose forensics}
On sorghum the three-stream arm beats every published frozen encoder
(EmbodiedMAE-B~\citep{dong2025embodiedmae}, Point-MAE~\citep{pang2022pointmae},
DINOv2~\citep{oquab2024dinov2}, MultiMAE~\citep{bachmann2022multimae}) on
every trait, and EmbodiedMAE-B, the strongest, on eight of nine paired over
plants (size $+0.004$ to $+0.007$, roll $+0.04$, leaf length $+0.08$, leaf
width $+0.09$); it also beats a network of our architecture trained from scratch
on the probe targets on seven of nine traits. On maize the four-stream arm beats
EmbodiedMAE-B on 10 of 11 traits and the best pose-free baseline per trait on
9 (val, point estimates), but the maize network trained from scratch is ahead of
every frozen 600-epoch arm on all eleven; the maize headline model closes most of
that gap. On the first maize release, supplying Point-MAE with each view's full
camera pose gave it the generator's fixed plant azimuth, a canonicalization that
no field rig provides; against Point-MAE given gravity alone (the information a
calibrated rig provides), our pose-free model won 5 of 9 decided targets. Tassel
droop is the weakest trait for every encoder of this design.

\subsection{Latent structure (E9)}
The latents are continua, as expected for a single-generator population: best
silhouette 0.15 to 0.24 at $k{=}2$, adjusted Rand index $\approx 0$ against
shape deciles, t-SNE trustworthiness 0.95 to 0.99. The participation ratio is
4.4 to 6.3 (of 768) in sorghum and 6.7 to 11.3 in maize, consistent with
maize's more independent generative factors, and the dominant principal
component of the sorghum latents is the plant's size. Linear decodability is
distinct from local embedding structure: the probe reads roll at $R^2$ 0.78
(PC+RGB+D, val) from a latent in which roll is barely organized locally. We
therefore use probes, not t-SNE smoothness, for representation claims on
continuous generators.

\section{Dataset Generation}
\label{app:data}

\FieldProc{} holds four synthetic sets: isolated single-plant captures of maize
and sorghum, and \emph{row views} in which the same plants stand in a crop row
between two neighbors and are photographed by a ground rover. All images are
$1024\times1024$ with a $40^\circ$ vertical field of view
($f_x=f_y=512/\tan20^\circ=1406.7$\,px, principal point at the center), in the
OpenGL convention ($y$ up, the camera looking down $-z$).

\subsection{Procedural plants}
\paragraph{Maize.} Plant $i$ of 15{,}000 is generated with seed $i$ by a
procedural maize generator whose traits are drawn from Gaussians (height
$0.832\pm0.104$\,m, leaf count $12.35\pm2.57$, base leaf length
$0.448\pm0.057$\,m, base leaf width $0.066\pm0.010$\,m, base leaf angle
$43.98^\circ\pm10.13^\circ$), coupled through four latent traits (vigor,
slenderness, posture, texture) with correlation strength 0.735, with bottom,
middle and top canopy curves and per-leaf jitter (scale 0.687). The plants are
0.62--1.44\,m tall (median 1.01\,m). The generator record is reduced to the
plant height, the leaf count and, per leaf, its distance along the stem,
length, width, azimuth, angle and droopiness; per-leaf curvature is added by
rebuilding the midrib B-spline (target elevation
$\text{angle}+\text{droopiness}\cdot t^2$) and integrating
$\theta=\int|\kappa|\,ds$ over the blade, $s\in[0.22,1]$, with the blade length
$S$ from 10-node Gauss--Legendre quadrature.

\paragraph{Sorghum.} Plant $i$ of 15{,}000 is generated with seed $i$ by the
EvoEngine sorghum generator. It draws the plant heading uniformly in
$[0^\circ,360^\circ)$, the stem tilt ($0\pm1.5^\circ$), the leaf count
($20\pm2$, clamped to $[2,128]$) and the internode length
($0.04\pm0.03$\,m), and sets the stem length to
$\text{internode}\times n_\text{leaves}/(1-s_0)$, with $s_0$ the first leaf's
starting point. Leaf $k$ at $t=k/(n-1)$ takes its starting point, length,
width, roll ($\text{roll}_k=(k\bmod2)\cdot180^\circ+\text{roll}(t)$), branching
angle, bending, curling and waviness from curves over $t$. No panicle is
generated. The plants have 4--35 leaves (mean 19.5) on stems of
0.19--1.74\,m.

\subsection{Splits}
Both species are split by plant into 10{,}500 / 2{,}250 / 2{,}250 plants.
\emph{Maize:} 19 scalar features are robustly standardized,
$z=(x-\mathrm{median})/(1.4826\,\mathrm{MAD})$, each plant is scored by
$\sqrt{z^\top(\Sigma+10^{-6}I)^{+}z}$, and plants are dealt from most to least
extreme alternately to test and validation until both hold 2{,}250 (seed 0).
Train scores span 1.56--4.60, validation 4.60--23.3, test 4.60--32.2.
\emph{Sorghum:} the extremeness reproduces, to the stored precision, as the
Mahalanobis distance from the mean (sample covariance) over leaf count, stem
length, mean and maximum leaf length, and mean and standard deviation of leaf
roll. The hold-out probability rises with a plant's extremeness rank, from 0.3\,\% in the least
extreme 5\,\% to 70\,\% in the most extreme 5\,\% (median rank 0.75 on validation
and test, 0.37 on train). \todo{This rule is inferred from the released
assignment; the script that wrote it was not found.}

\subsection{Isolated-plant captures}
Both species are rendered off-screen with \texttt{pyrender}. The camera looks at
the plant's bounding-box center $\mathbf c$ with world up $(0,1,0)$; lighting is
ambient 0.15 plus three white directional lights aimed at $\mathbf c$ from
$(1,1,1)$, $(-1,0.5,-0.5)$ and $(0,1,0)$ (intensities 4.0, 2.0, 1.5), on a gray
(0.5) background. Depth $z$ with near and far planes $n,f$ is stored as
$v=\lfloor\mathrm{clip}((z-n)/(f-n),0,1)(2^{32}-1)\rfloor$ across the four
8-bit channels of a PNG, most significant byte first; $v=0$ is background. A
pixel $(u,v)$ with depth $z>0$ back-projects to
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf p_{\mathrm{cam}}=\Big(\tfrac{u-c_x}{f_x}z,\;-\tfrac{v-c_y}{f_y}z,\;-z\Big),
  \label{eq:supp-backproject}
\end{equation}
and clouds are drawn uniformly without replacement from these points.

\emph{Maize:} ten view directions per plant are drawn uniformly on the sphere
(seeded per plant), at distance $1.05\,r/\sin20^\circ$ with $r$ half the
bounding-box diagonal; a fourth light (1.5) is placed at the camera, and textured
leaf, stem and tassel materials are double-sided. Each view stores RGB, an
RGBA image with the background cleared, depth, the camera pose, the trimmed
parameter file, and an 8{,}192-point cloud back-projected from that view's
depth (Eq.~\ref{eq:supp-backproject}), so the cloud contains only the surface
visible to the camera.
\emph{Sorghum:} the ten directions form a Fibonacci sphere,
$y_i=1-2(i+\tfrac12)/10$, $\phi_i=i(\sqrt5-1)\pi+\epsilon_i$ with a small fixed
jitter, identical for every plant (five above and five below the horizon), at
2.5\,m; one leaf texture covers the plant and there is no camera light. Each
view stores RGB, depth, the camera pose, the parameter file, and the complete,
occlusion-free labeled plant cloud moved into the camera frame.

\subsection{Row views}
\paragraph{Composition.} Within a split with plant list $L$, row $i$ has main
plant $L_i$, right neighbor $L_{i+1}$ and left neighbor $L_{i+2}$ (indices
modulo $|L|$), so every plant is a main plant once. The world frame is the main
plant's (stem base at the origin, row along $x$, rover on the $+z$ side), with
the neighbors' stem bases at $(\pm d,0,0)$, $d=0.15$\,m. Each row is rendered
at ten orientations: the main plant is turned about its stem base by
$\theta\in\{0^\circ,10^\circ,\dots,90^\circ\}$, each neighbor by
$\theta+\delta$, $\delta\sim U(-15^\circ,15^\circ)$ (seeded per split and row).
Sorghum stems lean, so each sorghum neighbor is also rotated about its stem
base by the smallest rotation that makes its stem axis (the principal axis of
its stem vertices) parallel to the main plant's; the main plant is never
tilted, and maize is not tilted.

\paragraph{Contact solver.} The three plants are made free of intersections
while staying as close as possible to their grown shapes; they may touch but
not cross. Each blade is a chain of 8\,cm segments from collar to tip whose
joints bend along circular arcs, up/down and sideways, so the blade stays
smooth, keeps its length and stays attached. Stems and sheaths (everything
within 2.5\,cm of the stem axis) and the maize tassel are rigid. A leaf's joint
bends sum to a total-turn limit, and a leaf may not bend into its own stem or
cross its own plant's leaves more than it does as grown. Where a leaf would
cross another plant, it bends at the joint before the contact by the smallest
clearing angle, smallest first, so it comes to rest on or under what it meets.
Neighbors bend first; a main-plant leaf bends only where a neighbor
cannot move (a neighbor's stem or sheath, or a neighbor leaf with no clear
shape). A sorghum neighbor leaf that cannot bend clear may also roll about its
stem by up to $\pm90^\circ$. Candidates are screened on 1\,cm occupancy grids and
every remaining contact is decided by exact triangle--triangle tests; a found
layout is then settled, each bent leaf easing back toward its grown shape to
the least bend that still clears. Unsolved layouts are retried under looser
limits (Tab.~\ref{tab:supp-stages}), then at spacings raised in 5\,cm steps up
to 1.5\,m and refined in 1\,cm steps.

only if no triangle of one plant intersects a triangle of another.

row, while its parameter record describes the grown plant; main-plant leaves
are bent only where unavoidable. At 2.5\,m the lens covers about 1.8\,m of

10 sample sorghum rows the main plant touches the top edge in 58 of 100 shots).
The two isolated sets differ in view sampling, camera distance, materials,
lighting and per-view cloud. Sorghum plant 0 uses seed 0, for which the
generator does not reseed. \todo{Check that plant 0's parameters match its mesh.}
