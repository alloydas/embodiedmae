\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Why a stream that holds the answer hurts.}
The parameter stream is the only input that states the phenotype verbatim, and
it is the one input the field never provides. Every arm that let the encoder
see it learned a worse sensor representation, and the loss grew with how often
it was visible (Tab.~\ref{tab:control}); even as a pure decoder target it cost
roll and leaf length, the two traits the leaf tokens spell out per leaf. The
reading consistent with modality competition and negative transfer is that a
privileged stream lets the encoder route a trait through tokens it will not
have at test time, and the reconstruction pressure of a per-leaf target then
pulls the latent toward the plant's size, the direction its first principal
component already carries. The practical rule the ablations support is that a
generative modality belongs in the data and in the targets rather than in the
encoder. Our headline model does not yet follow it: it was pretrained, and its
teacher distilled, with the parameters as an encoder input, so its frozen
representation is the one the four-stream arm measures, and a headline run at
$\rho_\theta{=}1$ is the direct test. We expect the rule to transfer to any
multimodal model with access to a program, a CAD sketch, or a simulator state,
and the gated ratio $\rho_\theta$ is a one-line way to test it.

\paragraph{Which metric decides.}
Chamfer and the frozen probe agree on the coarse ranking of arms and disagree on
the decisions a designer has to make (Fig.~\ref{fig:metrics}): depth, more
training plants, and the parameter stream each move Chamfer by tens of percent
and the probe by nothing, while a half-visible cloud moves Chamfer fivefold and
the probe by $0.03$. On slender geometry the two measure different things,
surface proximity and recovered structure, and we let the probe decide and
report Chamfer as the decoder's surface error. The probe has its own costs: it
needs labels and a held-out split, its $R^2$ is comparable between models on
one split but is not a portable absolute (our held-out plants are
extreme-enriched), and sorghum's four named traits collapse to two generator
variables, which is why maize is the species on which the claims about
independent traits rest. Training-side, the Sinkhorn term is our response to
Chamfer's blindness to a missing blade; its ablation is pending
(\todo{E7}).

\paragraph{From complete clouds to a camera.}
Everything was pretrained on complete clouds of isolated plants, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:regime2} shows what that leaves out. The information survives a
single camera's view, but the latent moves with coverage, so a readout
calibrated on complete scans is wrong on partial ones and must be refit; the
decoder reproduces what it is given and does not hallucinate the hidden half;
and viewpoint, not morphology, sets the difficulty of inverting a photograph.
The recipe is the output that tolerates this best: ten photographs of one plant
move its recovered insertion heights by a tenth of their spread, and a
re-rendered recipe is complete even where the cloud is not. The row-occluded
regime and the real scans (Secs.~\ref{sec:regime3} and \ref{sec:res-field})
are the two evaluations this paper still owes, and the one-camera transfer gap
is our stated prediction for the sim-to-real penalty before they land. A
generator also carries regularities nobody intended, such as the fixed leaf
azimuth that handed a pose-aware baseline a canonical orientation; benchmark
users should expect more of these.

\paragraph{What distillation does and does not buy.}
A full-modality teacher improves every single-sensor student and concentrates
the gain on the weakest sources and the hardest plants
(Tab.~\ref{tab:anysubset}, Fig.~\ref{fig:views}B), and supervising all four
targets beats a parameter specialist at parameter regression. It does not make
the model view-robust: the elevation curve moves down and keeps its shape, so
view robustness needs a different lever, most plausibly views as an explicit
augmentation axis during pretraining. It also showed that a field nothing can
predict is not free: the three waviness parameters of the first sorghum release
stayed at zero skill and grew more view-dependent under distillation, and the
final generator drops them. A parameterization meant to be regressed should be
pruned to what its sensors can determine.

\paragraph{Scope and compute.}
The ablations use one seed per arm; the run-to-run floor comes from a single
near-duplicate pair (3\,\% Chamfer, $0.004$ $R^2$), and the maize scaling
results are within it. Two generators and two species-specific models are
studied, and the 1{,}000-epoch headline model is trained once per species.
The procedural prior that makes inversion tractable also bounds it: a plant
the generator cannot express cannot be recovered, and an organ class the
generator renders without variation (tassel droop in maize is below zero for
every encoder of this design) is a limit of the data as much as of the model.
Row occlusion, real scans, and the \FieldProc{} release at 10K--100K plants
remain \todo{in progress}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\CropCode{} recovers a complete, editable procedural plant from whichever
sensor a field campaign produced, in one forward pass, by letting the generator
supply both the training data and the output representation. Its design
rests on two findings that we expect to outlast the application: the
generator's parameters are valuable targets and harmful inputs, and on slender
geometry a downstream probe, not Chamfer distance, is the metric that decides
what a modality is worth. The remaining distance to the field is measured, not
guessed: a single camera's partial view costs a refit readout and a known
transfer gap, and the row-occluded and real-scan evaluations will say whether
the generator's prior pays for the rest. \todo{Sharpen once E10 lands.}
