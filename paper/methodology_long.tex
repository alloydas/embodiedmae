\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

A plant $x$ is observed through up to four streams,
$\Mods=\{\rgb,\dep,\pc,\prm\}$: an RGB image $x^{\rgb}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times224\times224}$,
a depth map $x^{\dep}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times224\times224}$ from the same camera, a
point cloud $x^{\pc}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times3}$ ($N{=}8196$) expressed in that camera's
frame, and the \emph{procedural parameters} $x^{\prm}$ that generated the plant.
The first three are what a sensor rig can deliver in the field. The fourth exists
only in simulation: it is the recipe (stem length, stem direction, panicle
geometry, and per-leaf insertion height, length, roll, branching angle and blade
width) from which the mesh, and hence every image and scan of the plant, was
produced. Our premise is that a representation forced to \emph{explain} its visual
inputs in terms of this recipe is a better phenotyping representation than one
trained to reconstruct pixels and points alone, and that the recipe is a natural
target for inverse procedural modelling: given a photograph, recover the plant's
generative parameters in a single forward pass.

We cast all of this as one masked-modelling problem. Each modality $m$ is
tokenised into a sequence $T_m$ of $L_m$ tokens; a shared transformer encoder
$f_\phi$ reads the \emph{visible} subset of the concatenated tokens and a lighter
decoder $g_\psi$ reconstructs every token of every active modality. Writing
$\vis\subseteq\bigcup_m T_m$ for the visible set,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}^{m} = g_\psi\!\big(f_\phi(\vis)\big)_m, \qquad
  \mathcal{L}(\phi,\psi) = \sum_{m\in\Mods_{\mathrm{act}}} w_m\,\ell_m\!\big(\hat{x}^{m},x^{m}\big),
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $\Mods_{\mathrm{act}}\subseteq\Mods$ is the set of modalities a given arm is
built with and $\ell_m$ is a modality-specific reconstruction loss
(Sec.~\ref{sec:pretrain}). The choice of $\vis$ is the whole design space:
\emph{pretraining} draws it at random across modalities, so that the model learns
to complete any stream from any other; \emph{distillation} fixes it to a single
sensor stream, so that a deployed model can run from one camera; and
\emph{probing} fixes it to the sensor streams only, so that the procedural
parameters, which no field sensor observes, can never leak into a phenotype
estimate. Throughout, $x^{\prm}$ is treated as an \emph{input} only during
pretraining; at evaluation and deployment it is always a target.

Downstream, the quantity of interest is phenotype $y$ (height, leaf count, a
biomass proxy, leaf angle, leaf length, leaf width). We read it from the frozen
encoder's CLS token by ridge regression (Sec.~\ref{sec:field}), so the comparison
between arms is a comparison of representations, not of decoders.

\subsection{A procedural field-data engine}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Generator.}
Every plant is a draw from a single procedural sorghum generator
(\texttt{SorghumGenerator/Random.sg}) seeded with the plant index, so the
population is one continuous distribution with no discrete genotype variable.
Each draw emits a textured mesh (stem plus a double-shell $u$--$v$ grid per leaf)
and a parameter record: five plant-level quantities (stem length and direction,
panicle size, seed count and radius) and, per leaf, the insertion point along the
stem, length, roll angle, branching angle and blade width, the last measured on the
mesh as the maximal margin-to-margin distance over the blade. The generator couples
some of these: stem length is $0.05\,n_{\mathrm{leaves}}-0.001$ ($R^2{=}0.988$), so
height and leaf count are one latent variable, and the branching angle varies by
only $0.27^\circ$ across the population. We report this explicitly because it
bounds what any phenotype probe on this data can show
(Sec.~\ref{sec:field}).\footnote{Leaf \emph{roll} is a near-uniform circular draw per
leaf; its per-plant mean is the only probe target uncorrelated with plant size.}

\paragraph{Rendering.}
We render with an offscreen OpenGL rasteriser (pyrender/trimesh, EGL): $1024^2$
images, vertical field of view $40^\circ$, one shared leaf texture under a
metallic-roughness material, three fixed directional lights plus ambient, and a
uniform grey background. Ten cameras per plant sit on a Fibonacci sphere of radius
$2.5$\,m centred on the mesh bounding box, at elevations
$\pm64.2^\circ,\pm44.4^\circ,\pm30.0^\circ,\pm17.5^\circ,\pm5.7^\circ$ with
golden-angle azimuths and a $\pm0.05$\,rad jitter; the rig seed is fixed, so every
plant sees the same ten poses. Depth is stored losslessly as
$(d-0.025)/(50-0.025)$ clipped to $[0,1]$ and packed into 32-bit big-endian RGBA.
For each view we also export the complete plant cloud (${\sim}75$k points, sampled
from the mesh) rigidly transformed into that camera's frame, together with the
camera pose. The cloud is therefore the \emph{whole} plant, not a scan from that
camera; Sec.~\ref{sec:field} describes how we simulate the partial clouds a rig
would capture. A post-processing pass solves a straight alpha matte from the depth
mask and the known background colour, so RGB can be composited over any background
(we train on black).

\paragraph{Scale and split.}
The corpus is $15{,}000$ plants $\times$ 10 views $=150{,}000$ samples, with
4--35 leaves per plant and stem lengths of 0.19--1.74\,m. We split \emph{by
plant}, 70/15/15, and deliberately enrich the held-out splits with unusual
plants: each plant's \emph{extremeness} is its Mahalanobis distance from the
population centroid in six standardised features (leaf count, stem length, mean and
max leaf length, mean and s.d.\ of leaf roll); plants are drawn into val+test
without replacement with probability $\propto(\mathrm{rank}+0.02)^2$ and the pool
is halved (seed 42). The result is 10{,}500 / 2{,}250 / 2{,}250 plants with median
extremeness 2.03 on train versus 2.76 on val and test, and 4.7\,\% versus
22--23\,\% of plants in the top extremeness decile. Held-out $R^2$ is thus
comparable \emph{between} models on a split but is not a portable absolute
number; we report native-unit MAE alongside it.

\paragraph{Views as augmentation (one plant set, two camera sets).}
An epoch visits every training plant once with \emph{one} randomly chosen view,
drawn from a hash of (view seed, epoch, plant) so that all data workers and all
GPUs agree. Every ablation arm shares the view seed and therefore sees the same
views in the same order. Validation and test always use view 00. Data-scaling arms
take nested random subsets of plants (1k $\subset$ 3k $\subset$ 10k) with all ten
views, so a step along the data axis never changes the augmentation regime.

\paragraph{Loader.}
RGB is alpha-composited over black, resized to $224^2$ and ImageNet-normalised.
Depth is decoded from its packed representation to $[0,1]$ (background $=0$) and
resized. The cloud is subsampled without replacement to $N{=}8196$ points (padded
with replacement if short), centred at its centroid and scaled to the unit sphere.
The parameter record becomes $1+K$ tokens of nine floats, $K{=}24$: token~0 is the
plant, $[\,\text{stem length},\ \text{stem dir}_{xyz},\ \text{panicle}_{xyz},\
\text{seeds},\ \text{seed radius}\,]$; tokens $1\ldots K$ are leaves,
$[\,\text{insertion},\ \text{length},\ \text{roll},\ \text{branch},\ \text{width},\
0,0,0,0\,]$. Each field is affinely mapped to $[0,1]$ by a fixed scale and shift
(e.g.\ length$/1.25$, roll$/360^\circ$, branch$/180^\circ$, width$/0.2$,
$(\text{dir}+1)/2$) and clipped; a validity mask marks real leaf tokens and zeros
pad the rest. Plants with more than $K$ leaves (1{,}586 of 15{,}000) lose their
excess leaf tokens. \todo{decide whether to raise $K$ or state the truncation as a
limitation.}

\subsection{Four-stream masked pretraining}
\label{sec:pretrain}

\paragraph{Tokenisers.}
RGB and depth are patchified by a stride-16 convolution into $14\times14=196$
tokens each, with fixed 2-D sine--cosine positional embeddings shared between the
two image streams. The cloud is grouped Point-MAE style: farthest-point sampling
picks 196 centres (random start), $k$NN gathers 32 neighbours per centre, each
group is expressed relative to its centre and passed through a two-stage
mini-PointNet (Conv$_{3\to128\to256}$, max-pool, concatenate the pooled global
feature, Conv$_{512\to512\to D}$, max-pool) to one token per group. PC tokens
receive a learned positional table indexed by FPS order. The parameter stream uses
no text model: each nine-float token is projected by
$\mathrm{LN}(\mathrm{GELU}(W\,p+b))$ with its own learned positional table (slot~0
the plant, slots $1\ldots K$ the leaves). Every token receives a learned
per-modality embedding; a learned CLS token is prepended. Four backbone sizes share
this skeleton (Table~\ref{tab:sizes}); an inactive modality has no tokeniser, no
head and no loss, it is absent rather than masked.

\paragraph{Dirichlet allocation with exact budgets.}
Let $L_m$ be the token count of modality $m$ and $\rho{=}0.80$ the total mask
ratio. The visible budget is $V=\lfloor(1-\rho)\sum_m L_m\rfloor$ (117 tokens for
three $196$-token streams). Once per forward pass we draw
$\lambda\sim\mathrm{Dirichlet}(\alpha\mathbf{1})$, $\alpha{=}1$, over the budgeted
modalities and allocate $V$ in proportion to $\lambda$ by capped water-filling with
largest-remainder rounding, subject to $1\le n_m\le\lfloor L_m(1-\rho_{\min})\rfloor$
with $\rho_{\min}{=}0.25$: every modality keeps at least one visible token and loses
at least a quarter. The counts $n_m$ sum to $V$ exactly and are shared by the
batch, so no sample ever has a zero-token modality and no padding enters the
encoder; \emph{which} positions are visible is drawn per sample by a uniform
shuffle. This exact-budget allocator follows EmbodiedMAE's fixed-visible-count
scheme \todo{cite} and departs from it only in the floor $\rho_{\min}$.

The parameter stream is a special case. Left inside the shared budget, its 25
short tokens are on average 59\,\% visible at $\rho{=}0.80$ while the vision
streams fall from 20\,\% to 18\,\%, i.e.\ adding the fourth stream silently taxes
the other three. We therefore gate it: a separate ratio $\rho_\prm$ fixes
$n_\prm=\max(1,\lfloor(1+K)(1-\rho_\prm)\rfloor)$ visible parameter tokens, and the
Dirichlet budget is allocated over the vision streams alone, which makes the
vision masks of a four-stream arm bit-identical to those of its three-stream
sibling under the same seed. Setting $\rho_\prm{=}1$ removes the parameters from
the input altogether and keeps them only as a target. Our controlled ablation runs
$\rho_\prm\in\{\text{shared},0.80,0.40,1.0\}$.

\paragraph{Decoder and heads.}
Encoder outputs are projected to the decoder width, the CLS dropped, and every
modality's full sequence restored in canonical order (rgb, depth, pc, $\prm$;
$196{+}196{+}196{+}25=613$ positions) with a learned mask token in the hidden
slots and fixed decoder positional embeddings. Because the layout never depends on
the mask, teacher and student decoder sequences in Sec.~\ref{sec:distill} align
position for position. After eight pre-LN blocks, per-modality heads read out:
linear $16^2\!\cdot\!3$ and $16^2\!\cdot\!1$ values per image patch; for the cloud a
FoldingNet-style head that concatenates each of the 196 token features (projected
to 512) with a point on a $7\times7$ grid in $[-1,1]^2$ and folds it through a
three-layer MLP to $\lfloor N/196\rfloor{=}41$ points, i.e.\ the \emph{entire}
$N$-point cloud is regenerated regardless of which PC tokens were visible; and for
the parameters a two-layer MLP to nine unbounded floats per token.

\paragraph{Losses.}
With $M_m$ the binary mask of modality $m$ (1 = hidden), the per-stream terms in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} are
\begin{align}
  \ell_{\rgb} &= \frac{\sum_i M^{\rgb}_i\,\big\|\hat{p}_i-\tilde{p}_i\big\|_2^2/|p_i|}{\max(\sum_i M^{\rgb}_i,1)},
  \qquad \tilde{p}_i=\frac{p_i-\mu_i}{\sqrt{\sigma_i^2+10^{-6}}}, \\
  \ell_{\dep} &= \text{as }\ell_{\rgb}\text{ on } \tilde{d}=\frac{d-\min d}{\max(\max d-\min d,\,10^{-6})}
  \ \text{(per image)}, \\
  \ell_{\pc} &= \frac1N\sum_{i}\sigma\!\big(a(d_i-\tau)\big)\,d_i
             + \frac1N\sum_{j}\sigma\!\big(a(e_j-\tau)\big)\,e_j,
  \label{eq:qal} \\
  \ell_{\prm} &= \frac{\sum_t M^{\prm}_t\,v_t\,\overline{\mathrm{SmoothL1}}_{\beta=0.1}(\hat{\theta}_t,\theta_t)}{\max(\sum_t M^{\prm}_t v_t,1)} .
\end{align}
$\ell_{\rgb}$ is the per-patch-normalised MSE of MAE; $\ell_{\dep}$ is the same on a
per-image min--max-normalised target, so the model predicts normalised depth
directly. $\ell_{\pc}$ is a \emph{quality-aware} Chamfer: $d_i$ and $e_j$ are the
Euclidean (not squared) nearest-neighbour distances from prediction to target and
back, each weighted by a sigmoid gate $\sigma(a(\cdot-\tau))$ with $\tau{=}0.01$ and
$a{=}100$ that down-weights residuals already under one centimetre on the unit
sphere and concentrates the gradient on the thin-leaf geometry that plain Chamfer
neglects \todo{cite the QAL source}. It is computed on the full regenerated cloud.
$\ell_{\prm}$ is averaged over the nine slots and counted only on tokens that are
both hidden ($M^{\prm}_t{=}1$) and real ($v_t{=}1$). Weights are
$w_{\rgb}{=}w_{\dep}{=}1$, $w_{\pc}{=}1$ and $w_{\prm}{=}5$. Because the sum runs over
$\Mods_{\mathrm{act}}$, totals are not comparable across arms with different
modality sets; we compare arms by downstream probes and by per-modality metrics,
never by total loss.

\paragraph{Schedule.}
AdamW ($\beta{=}(0.9,0.95)$, weight decay 0.05), linear warm-up then half-cosine
decay to zero, gradient-norm clipping at 1.0, mixed modality masking at every step.
The headline model (E1) trains the \emph{base} backbone on all 105{,}000 views per
epoch for 1{,}000 epochs at global batch 256 (lr $6\times10^{-4}$, 20 warm-up
epochs), where global batch is per-GPU batch $\times$ GPUs $\times$ accumulation
steps; we keep the per-GPU batch at 32 across hardware because the PointNet
tokeniser's BatchNorm statistics are not synchronised across ranks. Ablation arms
(modality, data scale, model scale) use the view-sampling regime above at global
batch 32 and are matched in \emph{optimiser steps}, not epochs: at one view per
plant an epoch is the plant count, so a 1k-plant arm runs 6{,}169 epochs, a 3k arm
2{,}100 and a 10k arm 631 to reach the same $197{,}400$ updates as a 600-epoch
full-data arm. Warm-up, validation and checkpoint frequencies scale by the same
factor.

\subsection{A vision-complete teacher}
\label{sec:distill}

A field model must run from whatever sensor is present, which the pretraining
objective does not guarantee: at $\rho{=}0.80$ the encoder has never seen a single
modality standing alone with the others entirely absent. We close that gap by
self-distillation from a frozen copy of the pretrained model that \emph{is} shown
everything.

The teacher is the pretrained checkpoint with the lowest validation loss (epoch
960 of 1{,}000 for the headline model), frozen and run in evaluation mode. It
receives all four streams with no masking at all, $\vis=\bigcup_m T_m$, and emits
three things: the encoder CLS latent $c^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$, the full
613-position decoder feature sequence $F^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{613\times512}$ taken
after the final decoder LayerNorm, and (optionally) its reconstructions. Only the
first two are used. Because the teacher sees the parameters, $F^{T}$ at the
parameter positions is a vision-and-recipe-informed target for a student that will
be shown pixels alone; and because the decoder layout is mask-independent, every
one of the 613 positions has a student counterpart.

Two remarks on the teacher regime, both verified in our runs. First, an
all-visible teacher is \emph{out of distribution} for a model pretrained at
$\rho_{\min}{=}0.25$; on the sorghum headline model the cost is small (full-cloud
Chamfer $4.8\times10^{-4}$ all-visible versus $3.5\times10^{-4}$ at 50\,\% visible
per stream), but on a second species it was catastrophic (the cloud collapsed,
Chamfer $0.05$--$0.09$), and there we distil from a teacher shown half of each
stream's tokens instead. The teacher's own reconstruction quality should be
measured before distilling from it. Second, distillation's value shrinks as the
teacher improves: against a weak early teacher the same recipe cut cross-modal
generation loss by 54\,\%; against the headline model it cuts 19\,\%
(Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). \todo{confirm the final numbers against the $d2$ rerun.}

\subsection{Single-sensor students}
\label{sec:students}

The student is initialised from the same checkpoint as the teacher and trained
for 100 epochs (AdamW, lr $10^{-4}$, 5 warm-up epochs, cosine, global batch 128)
on a mixture of two step types chosen by a seeded coin so that all GPUs agree:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cross-modal steps} (probability $p_{\mathrm{xm}}{=}0.8$). A single
  source modality $s$ is selected, cycling deterministically through
  $(\rgb,\dep,\pc,\prm)$ across steps; $\vis=T_s$ and the other three streams
  contribute no tokens to the encoder (their masks are all ones, so the decoder
  rebuilds them from mask tokens alone). Optionally a fraction $\rho_s$ of the
  source's \emph{own} tokens is hidden as well (\emph{source masking};
  $\rho_s{=}0.5$ in our specialist arms, 0 in the generalist), which turns the
  source into a reconstruction target too and prevents the student from learning
  a whole-image shortcut. The loss is
  \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{xm}} = \sum_{m\in\Mods_{\mathrm{act}}} w_m\,\ell_m
      \;+\; \lambda_F\,\frac{\sum_{j} u_j\,\big\|F^{S}_j-F^{T}_j\big\|_2^2/512}{\sum_j u_j}
      \;+\; \lambda_c\,\big\|c^{S}-c^{T}\big\|_2^2/D ,
    \label{eq:distill}
  \end{equation}
  with $\lambda_F{=}1$, $\lambda_c{=}0.5$, teacher terms detached, and $u_j{=}1$ on
  the positions of the three \emph{generated} modalities and $0$ on the source's
  own positions (or $1$ on a single target modality's positions when a specialist
  is trained with a declared target). The first term is ground-truth
  reconstruction of all four streams from the one that was shown, i.e.\ the
  student is asked to regenerate the depth map, the full cloud and the
  procedural recipe from an RGB image. Output-level distillation
  (matching the teacher's reconstructions) is implemented but switched off.
\item \textbf{Full-reconstruction steps} (probability $1-p_{\mathrm{xm}}$). An
  ordinary Dirichlet-masked pretraining step at $\rho{=}0.80$ with no distillation
  terms. One such step in five is what keeps the non-target heads alive: with
  $p_{\mathrm{xm}}{=}1$ the depth head of an RGB$\to$PC specialist collapsed by
  $7\times$ within ten epochs, and $p_{\mathrm{xm}}{=}0.8$ repaired it in one epoch
  and held it.
\end{itemize}

Under gradient accumulation the step type and source are keyed on the optimiser
step, so an accumulation group is homogeneous and the effective global batch is
not mixed. The generalist uses $\vis\in\{T_\rgb,T_\dep,T_\pc,T_\prm\}$; specialists
fix the source list to one stream and may declare a target (e.g.\ RGB$\to$cloud,
RGB$\to$parameters, cloud$\to$parameters), in which case $\sum_m w_m\ell_m$ and
$u_j$ are restricted to that target and the model-selection metric is that
target's own generation quality (Chamfer for the cloud, parameter MAE for the
recipe).

\paragraph{Evaluation of generation.}
Every $k$ epochs and at the end we run each source alone over 1{,}600 held-out
plants with the source \emph{unmasked} ($\rho_s{=}0$, so source masking is a pure
training-time regulariser and all arms are scored on identical footing) and report,
per source: masked RGB and depth MSE, Chamfer between the generated and true cloud,
and MAE of the generated parameters over real leaf tokens. We always report
per-source numbers and the \emph{epoch-0} numbers of the undistilled warm start,
measured by the same routine, because the first epoch of distillation already
captures much of the gain for some sources and \emph{costs} ground for others
(RGB$\to$parameters was 6.8\,\% worse than its warm start after one epoch and did
not cross back under it until epoch 7).

\subsection{Crossing into the field}
\label{sec:field}

Three properties separate field deployment from the synthetic regime above, and
the protocol addresses each.

\paragraph{The recipe is never observed.}
All downstream phenotype estimates use the frozen encoder's CLS token under
$\vis=\{T_\rgb,T_\dep,T_\pc\}\cap\Mods_{\mathrm{act}}$ with the parameter tensor
\emph{zeroed}, and no source masking. This is not a convenience: the plant token's
first slot is stem length, i.e.\ the height target verbatim, and a probe that let
an arm see it would be handed the answer. (We verified the leak is structurally
impossible: features with real and zeroed parameters differ by exactly 0.) A
side-effect is that the three- and four-stream arms are probed on an identical
589-token input, so the only difference between them is what pretraining taught
the encoder. The probe itself is ridge regression on one deterministic view per
plant (ten views share a label, so view rows would inflate $n$ tenfold and shrink
every error bar by $\sim3.2\times$), with the standardiser fit on train only and
$R^2$ reported on the extreme-enriched val and test plants; paired bootstrap over
plants (2{,}000 resamples) gives confidence intervals on every between-arm
difference, and a "win" requires the 95\,\% CI to exclude zero on \emph{both}
splits. Feature extraction is stochastic in two places even under
\texttt{no\_grad} (FPS's first centroid and the cloud subsample), so both are
seeded. Point targets are height, leaf count, the biomass proxy
$n_{\mathrm{leaves}}\times\overline{\text{leaf length}}$, mean leaf roll, mean and
max leaf length, and mean and max blade width; because the generator ties height
to leaf count, the four named 6.4 targets have rank two, and we print each target's
correlation with leaf count beside its score.

\paragraph{A camera sees part of a plant.}
Every training cloud is the complete plant moved into the camera frame. To ask
what a model loses when its cloud is what a rig would capture, we cast a ray from
each camera to every point's nearest mesh location and mark the point visible if
the ray is unobstructed to within 0.5\,mm and the point lies inside the camera
frustum ($f\approx1406$\,px at $1024^2$, fitted from the rendered silhouettes;
checked against the renderer: 95\,\% of visible points land on the silhouette and
71\,\% match its depth within 5\,mm, against 3\,\% of occluded points). Camera 00
alone covers $48.8\pm7\,\%$ of a plant; cameras 00+01+02 cover $78.3\pm4\,\%$. We
then score each arm under full, three-camera and one-camera clouds in two ways:
\emph{matched}, refitting the probe per condition, which measures information
retained; and \emph{transfer}, applying the full-cloud probe to partial clouds,
which measures what a readout calibrated on complete scans does on real ones.
Reconstruction under partial input is scored against the complete cloud and must
be run in the masked regime (visible streams token-masked at the training
$\rho$): with every token visible the decoder is out of distribution and the cloud
collapses by an order of magnitude, the same failure that makes an all-visible
teacher unsafe (Sec.~\ref{sec:distill}).

\paragraph{One sensor at a time.}
The deployed models are the single-sensor students of Sec.~\ref{sec:students}
run with $\vis=T_s$ for whichever stream the rig provides. We score generation on
held-out plants against two floors: the Chamfer between a plant's cloud and a
\emph{different} sorghum plant's cloud (the "another plant" floor, $\approx0.049$),
and the distance to the dataset-mean cloud; skill is $1-\text{model}/\text{floor}$.
Chamfer is split into prediction$\to$truth (accuracy) and truth$\to$prediction
(completeness) because the two fail differently on partial inputs. On the
extreme-enriched test split the RGB-only student reaches $7.9\times10^{-4}$
(98.4\,\% skill), the depth-only student $6.2\times10^{-4}$, with a val$\to$test gap
under 2\,\%, i.e.\ no degradation from the enriched tails. We also report
generation error as a function of camera elevation, which is U-shaped and
symmetric about the horizon: steep views from above \emph{or} below are hardest.

Two caveats carry through every field claim. The renderer's three fixed lights,
single leaf texture and grey background are a narrow appearance distribution, and
RGB is the stream our ablations identify as carrying the phenotype; the transfer
to field appearance is therefore the open question, not the geometry. And a
readout fitted on complete clouds is biased on partial ones (our transfer probe
