CVPR 28592 · Section 4.8, real-world validation

Real plants in crop rows, before and after fine-tuning on real data

The row model was fine-tuned on 75% of the real plants of each species and scored on the other 25%, which it never saw. Both figures show those held-out plants.

Figure 1 · real maize rows

What each row isOne camera stop on a held-out plant, two per layout (parallel and perpendicular), picked at the real-row fine-tune's median Chamfer, so it shows a typical case.
What changes across the last three columnsThe headline model writes a blurred plant; the row fine-tune drops the neighbors; the real-row fine-tune recovers the leaf layout and the height (for example 1.74 m against a true 1.70 m).

Figure 2 · real sorghum rows

Why the tiles are tallEach sorghum frame is a strip 7–17° wide and 61–76° high, framed to the whole plant. The plants are 2.2–5.3 m tall; training stems reach 1.74 m.
What the fine-tune fixes and what it does notWithout real data every model writes a short, dense plant. After 19 training plants the model recovers the stalk over the full height, but few of the leaves.

Every input improvesEach single stream gains, on both species; all three streams together stay best.
Layout and occlusionParallel rows stay easier than perpendicular ones (15.6 against 23.9 after). Across the four occlusion quartiles the fine-tuned Chamfer stays between 18.5 and 21.6.
HeightBefore, the model writes about 1.03 m for every plant. After, it follows the true height up to 2.0 m, where the recipe's height range ends; above that the error is 5.9%.
